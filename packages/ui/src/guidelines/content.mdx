# Content

We write for someone who is discovering the application, maybe on a small screen, maybe in a hurry. Words matter as much as visuals. These rules apply to the interface copy whatever its language.

## Tone

- Formal address, short sentences, everyday words. A LETS is an association of neighbors: the tone is warm without being familiar.
- Refer to members by their first and last name, and to things by their name in the application (request, event, information, exchange, units).
- No technical jargon: "Unable to connect, check your internet access" rather than "Network error 503". The exception is an unexpected error, whose technical message is shown under a plain title (see "Failing" in the patterns).
- No emoji in the interface.

## Buttons and links

- A verb that says what will happen: "Post the request", "Send 20 units", "Offer my help".
- Never "OK", "Submit" or "Click here" alone.
- The label of a confirmation button repeats the action of the dialog's title.

## Forms

- Label: a short noun ("Request title"), without a colon.
- Hint: a concrete example ("for example, Help to put up a shelf").
- Error: what is wrong and how to fix it, in one sentence. "Enter a number of units between 1 and 500" rather than "Invalid value".
- Placeholder: optional, never required to understand the field, never an instruction.

## Numbers, dates, currency

- The currency name comes from the instance configuration (`{currency}`, `{currencyPlural}`); "units" in this system stands for that name.
- Amounts use tabular figures, with the sign for a movement ("+ 20 units", "− 15 units").
- Relative dates for recent things ("2 hours ago", "yesterday"), then the full date ("Saturday, October 12 at 10 am").

## Empty screens and errors

- An empty screen says what will appear here and offers the first action.
- An error does not blame and always offers a way forward (retry, go back, contact the coordinator of the LETS).

## The instance

The name, city and tagline of a LETS are never hard-coded: they come from the instance configuration.
